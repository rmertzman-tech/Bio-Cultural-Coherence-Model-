🧠 Bio-Cultural Coherence Model (BCCM) Simulator

Integrative Research Tool for Understanding Institutional Impact on Human Flourishing

Ethics Beyond Boundaries | St. Petersburg College

RESEARCH FRAMEWORK NOTICE: This simulator implements the Bio-Cultural Coherence Model (BCCM), an integrative research framework synthesizing established findings from multiple disciplines: Sapolsky's stress physiology (double-blind empirical research on glucocorticoid effects), Greene's moral psychology (fMRI studies of moral reasoning), and World Values Survey data (cross-cultural empirical surveys). This is NOT a clinically validated diagnostic instrument. No direct double-blind experiments on the integrated BCCM framework have been completed, though validation studies are planned. This is a visualization and educational tool that breaks down disciplinary silos to show how cumulative knowledge from biology, psychology, and sociology can work together. The power comes from integration— showing mechanisms that individual disciplines study separately. The FHRI scores represent theoretical predictions based on integrated research, not clinical diagnoses. What you see here represents our best current synthesis of peer-reviewed research into how institutional design affects human capacity for moral reasoning and long-term wellbeing. This framework will evolve as we refine parameters and conduct validation research.

📊 Research Status & Methodology

What This Tool Is:

  • Integrative Research Framework: Synthesizes established findings from neuroscience (Sapolsky), moral psychology (Greene), sociology (Inglehart/WVS), and organizational theory into a unified agent-based model.
  • Educational Visualization Tool: Helps students understand complex interactions between institutional design, biological stress responses, cultural values, and moral reasoning capacity.
  • Theory Development Platform: Allows exploration of theoretical predictions that emerge when we integrate findings across traditionally siloed disciplines.
  • Work in Progress: Parameters are calibrated from published research where available, but the integrated model itself is under continuous refinement and development.

What This Tool Is NOT:

  • Not a Clinical Diagnostic: This is not validated for individual diagnosis, treatment planning, or clinical decision-making. Do not use FHRI scores as medical/psychological assessments.
  • Not Experimentally Validated (Yet): While individual components draw on peer-reviewed research, the integrated BCCM framework has not undergone direct double-blind experimental validation. Validation studies are planned.
  • Not Predictive for Individuals: Population-level patterns may not apply to specific persons. Individual variation is enormous and context-dependent.

Empirical Foundations:

Biological Components (Sapolsky)
Based on decades of glucocorticoid research, HPA axis studies, and PFC/amygdala neuroimaging. Stress → cortisol → PFC atrophy pathway is well-established in literature.
Moral Psychology (Greene)
Dual-process theory of moral judgment supported by fMRI studies showing DLPFC activation in deliberative reasoning vs. amygdala in automatic/emotional responses.
Cultural Values (Inglehart/WVS)
World Values Survey data (100+ countries, 400,000+ respondents) documenting survival vs. self-expression value shifts in response to existential security changes.
Moral Injury Research
Clinical literature on moral injury in healthcare (Litz et al.), military (Shay), and organizational contexts. Documented long-term psychological impacts of values violations.
The Integration Is The Innovation: Individual disciplines have studied stress physiology, moral reasoning, cultural values, and institutional effects separately. This model attempts to show how they interact as a dynamic system. The hypotheses generated here—like how institutional design affects population-level capacity for democratic deliberation via biological pathways—can guide future empirical research.

📡 Real-Time Biological & Cultural State

FHRI (Functional Health Resilience Index) Over Time

The FHRI is a research-based composite measure (0-100 scale) integrating biological capacity, phenomenological coherence, moral debt, and environmental fit. Based on theoretical synthesis of established research findings, not clinical validation. Thresholds below 50 suggest theoretical conditions where integrated functioning becomes unsustainable. Watch how different institutional designs produce divergent trajectories.

Biological State Dynamics (Based on Sapolsky's Research)

Glucocorticoid Load (L_GC): Modeled chronic stress accumulation based on HPA axis research | PFC Control Capacity (C_PFC): Theoretical capacity for deliberative reasoning based on stress-PFC atrophy relationship | Amygdala Sensitivity (S_Amyg): Modeled threat reactivity based on stress sensitization research. These represent theoretical dynamics extrapolated from empirical neuroscience, not direct measurements.

Moral Debt Accumulation (Theoretical Construct)

Moral debt is a theoretical construct representing accumulated psychological burden from values violations. Like financial debt, the model includes compounding effects (rumination, unprocessed trauma). The framework predicts that institutional pressure to violate values creates psychological costs that cannot be "discharged" externally—they must be metabolized through BioBond (somatic processing), Civic (narrative reconstruction), or Institutional (structural change) interventions. The "bankruptcy threshold" at 10 represents a theoretical point where repair capacity is overwhelmed. This draws on moral injury literature but represents our theoretical integration, not validated measurement.

Cultural Value Evolution (Based on World Values Survey Patterns)

Survival ← → Self-Expression values: Inglehart's WVS research documents population-level value shifts in response to existential security changes. Our model incorporates hysteresis—the empirically observed pattern where values regress toward survival mode faster than they recover. We model regression at rate α=0.15 and recovery at β=0.02 (5-10x slower), based on WVS longitudinal data patterns. This theoretical parameterization predicts that stress-induced value changes persist long after environmental conditions improve—a testable hypothesis about cultural resilience.

Decision Mode Distribution (Dual-Process Model)

Based on Greene's dual-process theory of moral judgment: when Expected Value of Control (EVC) becomes negative (biological/cultural costs exceed expected benefits), agents theoretically shift from MANUAL (deliberative, PFC-mediated) to AUTOMATIC (emotional, amygdala-driven) reasoning. This represents when stress-induced PFC impairment makes nuanced moral reasoning biologically costly. The model predicts this is when tribal/emotional responses dominate—a theoretical mechanism linking individual neuroscience to population-level polarization. Empirical validation of this threshold needed.

Population Lambda Coefficient (Theoretical Capacity for Deep Pragmatism)

Lambda represents the theoretical population-level capacity for complex moral reasoning, derived from mean PFC control capacity. This is a hypothesis about collective cognitive function: when mean C_PFC < 0.4, lambda approaches 0, suggesting the population may lose biological prerequisites for nuanced deliberation. This prediction emerges from integrating neuroscience (individual PFC function) with political theory (requirements for democratic reasoning). Empirical validation needed.

Constructor Loop Interventions

BioBond: Somatic repair (Schwartz Rounds, peer support, ritual) | Civic: Narrative reconstruction, collective meaning-making | Institutional: Structural change preventing future damage
